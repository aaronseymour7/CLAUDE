Abstract

Density-matrix renormalisation group (DMRG) gives a matrix-product state and the low-lying energies of a spin chain classically, but a compact trial circuit built from them keeps some excited-state weight. The projection filter of Stetcu, Baroni and Carlson removes it on a quantum computer using one ancilla, controlled time evolution and post-selection. We give a complete workflow around this filter, from the DMRG front end to a verified state, and evaluate it on the open J1J_1–J2J_2 spin-½ chain: N=4N=4–1616 with exact statevector simulation and real DMRG trial states compiled to circuits, and NN up to 32 in a DMRG/MPS-only regime where circuits are counted. The front end is accurate to 3.7e-13 against exact diagonalisation (N≤20N\le20) and reproducible to 1.2e-13 between independent runs (N≤32N\le32); the excited state is the lowest state of any symmetry (its symmetry is not assumed; it was a triplet in every exact case checked, N≤16N\le16, J2∈[0,1]J_2\in[0,1]), and the bandwidth is certified by Weyl’s inequality without a DMRG value for the top of the spectrum. The paper is organised around three questions. (1) Can the filter be certified without knowing the exact ground state? Yes, conditionally: an a-priori bound on the trace distance to the ground state, D≤ℓ‾+ϵT/pgD\le\sqrt{\bar\ell}+\epsilon_T/\sqrt{p_g}, needs only DMRG-derived inputs and held on all 99 simulated points. It is a conditional certificate: it assumes the DMRG shift, gap and ground-state weight are right, which DMRG cannot certify, and we state exactly how it changes when they are off by given amounts. It fails if the weight γ\gamma or the gap Δ\Delta is over-estimated (a gap 25%25\% too large already breaks it). (2) How conservative is the certificate? Its leakage term is reasonably tight (1.1–2.7×\times); its worst-case Trotter term is extremely conservative (9–77×\times, growing with NN), so the certified step count is 11–168×\times what is empirically sufficient. (3) Can the practical cost be recovered without the exact ground state? A hybrid rule, certified leakage plus a measured nn-versus-2n2n Trotter estimate, met every target (25 of 25) with 6–66×\times fewer steps than the certificate, but it is empirical, not rigorous. In gates, the hybrid filter costs 3×1023\times10^{2}–4×1044\times10^{4} CX including the trial circuit for N≤16N\le16, growing as roughly N2.5N^{2.5}–N3.1N^{3.1}, which is 28–150×\times the cost of preparing the state directly from the MPS at the same fidelity; the certified filter for N=16N=16–3232 needs 2.8×1062.8\times10^{6}–9.6×1079.6\times10^{7} CX (re-synthesised; 4.8×1064.8\times10^{6}–1.6×1081.6\times10^{8} direct) and 1.2×1061.2\times10^{6}–4.1×1074.1\times10^{7} non-Clifford rotations (direct). The workflow is a conditionally certified, reproducible way to use the filter and a calibrated account of its cost, not a resource advantage on these chains.

1. Introduction

A standard hybrid route to a ground state is to run DMRG, compile the resulting matrix-product state (MPS) into a shallow circuit, and repair the circuit’s residual error with a quantum projection step. The Stetcu–Baroni–Carlson (SBC) projection [1], related to the rodeo algorithm [2], multiplies the trial state’s energy components by a filter F(E)=∏icos(Eti+ϕi)F(E)=\prod_i\cos(Et_i+\phi_i) realised with pulses of controlled time evolution on a single ancilla. It sits among a family of ground-state projectors that use polynomial or Fourier filters of the Hamiltonian: phase-estimation-based preparation [3], quantum-eigenvalue-transformation filters [4,5] and, more generally, quantum singular value transformation. The SBC filter trades the optimal log(1/ε)\log(1/\varepsilon) scaling of those methods for a measurement-based, single-ancilla circuit whose filter can be designed and certified classically.

The practical difficulty is that the guarantee one would like, a bound on how far the prepared state is from the ground state, seems to need the ground state. This paper builds a workflow that avoids that, and asks three questions about it:

Question Short answer Where
Q1 Can SBC filtering be certified without knowing the exact ground state? Yes, conditionally on conservative bounds for the ground-state weight γ\gamma and the gap Δ\Delta. A certificate built from DMRG-derived inputs was never violated, and it fails if γ\gamma or Δ\Delta is over-estimated. Section 4
Q2 How conservative is that certificate? Leakage is reasonably tight; worst-case Trotter error is extremely conservative (and more so as NN grows). Section 5
Q3 Can we recover the practical cost without the exact ground state? Largely, with the nn-versus-2n2n hybrid rule, which met every target, but it is empirical rather than rigorous. Section 6

Section 2 specifies the workflow and the certificate (including exactly which inputs are rigorous and which are assumed), Section 3 the experiments, and Section 7 the remaining practical questions: which trial state to use, how sensitive the result is to design choices, what the whole thing costs in CX gates, including against preparing the state directly from the MPS, and what it costs for chains up to N=32N=32 where only DMRG and counted circuits are possible. Section 8 states the limitations. We do not claim a resource advantage; Section 7 shows there is none at these sizes.

2. The workflow

Stage 1 — classical front end (DMRG). From a Hamiltonian H=∑gHgH=\sum_gH_g (here the bonds of the chain) run two-site DMRG for the ground-state energy E0E_0 and a high-bond-dimension reference MPS ψ0\psi_0, and again for the first excitation E1E_1 with the penalty H+λ|ψ0⟩⟨ψ0|H+\lambda|\psi_0\rangle\langle\psi_0|, λ=1.1W\lambda=1.1W. The projector is applied as a rank-one term λ|w⟩⟨w|\lambda|w\rangle\langle w| in every two-site local problem, where ww is ψ0\psi_0 contracted with the current environments; this is the same penalty as adding the projector as an MPO (whose bond dimension is χ2\chi^2 and which ran out of memory beyond N=14N=14 at the tolerances used here) at O(χ3)O(\chi^3) cost. It returns the lowest state of any symmetry orthogonal to ψ0\psi_0, so the symmetry of the first excitation is not assumed. Exact diagonalisation (scripts/check_first_excitation.py) of open chains with N=8N=8–1616 at 22 values of J2∈[0,1]J_2\in[0,1] (step 0.05, plus 0.2411) finds a non-degenerate singlet ground state and a triplet first excitation in every case (lowest Sz=1S^z=1 level equals the second Sz=0S^z=0 level and lies below the lowest Sz=2S^z=2 level); this is a finite-size, finite-grid check, not a proof for larger NN. The critical point J2c≈0.2411J_2^c\approx0.2411 [12,13] is located by a singlet–triplet crossing in periodic chains. Stage 2 — trial state. Run DMRG at a small maximum bond dimension χ\chi and compile the MPS to a circuit (here exactly, by sequential isometries with mps-to-circuit; an approximate brickwork compilation is also tested). Estimate the ground-state weight γ=∥Π0ψ∥2\gamma=\|\Pi_0\psi\|^2 (the weight in the whole ground space; a single nondegenerate vector in the cases studied) as the overlap with the reference MPS, by contraction when NN is too large for a statevector. If 1−γ≤ε1-\gamma\le\varepsilon for the target ε\varepsilon, stop: the trial state already suffices.

Stage 3 — filter. One pulse (t,ϕ)(t,\phi) applies 𝖧ae−itHs⊗ZaRza(2ϕ)𝖧a\mathsf H_{\mathrm{a}}\,e^{-itH_s\otimes Z_{\mathrm{a}}}\,\mathrm{Rz}_{\mathrm{a}}(2\phi)\,\mathsf H_{\mathrm{a}}; its ancilla-|0⟩|0\rangle block is cos(Hst+ϕ)\cos(H_st+\phi). Two numbers characterise the filter: η=supE∈[Δ,1]|F(E)|/|F(0)|\eta=\sup_{E\in[\Delta,1]}|F(E)|/|F(0)| (suppression of excited states relative to the ground state) and pg=γF(0)2p_g=\gamma F(0)^2 (a lower bound on the success probability). We design (ti,ϕi)(t_i,\phi_i) with floor.py, which maximises F(0)2F(0)^2 subject to a certified bound on η\eta (interval bisection using |F′|≤∑|ti||F'|\le\sum|t_i| and |F″|≤(∑|ti|)2|F''|\le(\sum|t_i|)^2), for a leakage budget εℓ=ε/4\varepsilon_\ell=\varepsilon/4.

Stage 4 — Trotterisation and the choice of nn. Each controlled evolution is kik_i first-order Lie–Trotter steps over the bonds, n=∑ikin=\sum_ik_i, ti=kidtt_i=k_i\,dt. The Trotter error is governed by the commutator constant α=∑g∥[Hg,∑g′>gHg′]∥\alpha=\sum_g\big\|[H_g,\sum_{g'>g}H_{g'}]\big\| (the larger of the two product orders; the ancilla does not change it). It is evaluated exactly, with no further inequality: for Pauli strings [Pg,Pg′]=2PgPg′[P_g,P_{g'}]=2P_gP_{g'} when they anticommute and 00 otherwise, so each term is 2|cg|∥∑g′>g,anticomm.cg′Pg′∥2|c_g|\,\|\sum_{g'>g,\ \text{anticomm.}}c_{g'}P_{g'}\|, a tiny dense eigenvalue problem on the few qubits near term gg, with cost linear in the number of terms for any NN. Three rules choose nn:

  • Certified. The smallest nn with D‾(n)≤ε\bar D(n)\le\sqrt\varepsilon, where the certificate is D≤D‾:=ℓ‾+αT22nγF(0)2,ℓ‾=(1−γ)η2γ+(1−γ)η2\boxed{\;D\le\bar D:=\sqrt{\bar\ell}+\frac{\alpha T^2}{2n\sqrt{\gamma F(0)^2}},\qquad\bar\ell=\frac{(1-\gamma)\eta^2}{\gamma+(1-\gamma)\eta^2}\;} DD is the trace distance between the implemented and the exact ground state. The first term is leakage (what an exact filter would leave) and depends only on γ\gamma and η\eta; the second is the Trotter term. Equivalently, infidelity ≤D‾2\le\bar D^2 and energy error ⟨H⟩−E0≤WD‾2\langle H\rangle-E_0\le W\bar D^2. The proof (Appendix A) is the triangle inequality for the trace distance plus a leakage lemma and a first-order Trotter lemma.
  • Hybrid. Keep the rigorous leakage term but replace the worst-case Trotter term by a measured one. For each candidate nn on a geometric grid, run the same filter at nn and 2n2n steps; because the first-order error scales as 1/n1/n, the Trotter distance is ≈2D(φ̃n,φ̃2n)\approx2D(\tilde\varphi_n,\tilde\varphi_{2n}). Accept the smallest nn such that ℓ‾+2D(φ̃n,φ̃2n)≤ε\sqrt{\bar\ell}+2D(\tilde\varphi_n,\tilde\varphi_{2n})\le\sqrt\varepsilon at that grid point and the next. This uses no ground state and is not a theorem (it assumes the 1/n1/n regime), but it is what a practitioner can actually evaluate.
  • Measured (oracle). The smallest nn whose measured DD meets the target. It needs the exact ground state, so it is a reference, not a rule.

The certificate is conditional on the DMRG inputs, and this can be made explicit. Let Es=E0DMRGE_s=E_0^{\mathrm{DMRG}} be the shift, ε0=Es−E0≥0\varepsilon_0=E_s-E_0\ge0 (DMRG is variational), μ0=minφ⊥ψ0⟨φ|H|φ⟩\mu_0=\min_{\varphi\perp\psi_0}\langle\varphi|H|\varphi\rangle and ε1=E1DMRG−μ0\varepsilon_1=E_1^{\mathrm{DMRG}}-\mu_0, the optimisation error of the excited-state solve. Assume user-supplied bounds ε0≤ε‾0\varepsilon_0\le\bar\varepsilon_0, ε1≤ε‾1\varepsilon_1\le\bar\varepsilon_1 (neither is computable from DMRG alone). 4. Q1: Can the filter be certified without knowing the exact ground state?

Short answer: yes, conditionally. Every quantity in the certificate, γ\gamma, Δ\Delta, WW, η\eta, F(0)F(0), α\alpha and TT, can be computed from DMRG output and the circuit, with no access to the exact ground state. The certificate is then a theorem given γ\gamma and Δ\Delta (Appendix A), and it is only as safe as those two inputs.

8. Discussion

The three answers together. The filter can be certified without the exact ground state, as long as γ\gamma and Δ\Delta are not optimistic (Q1); the certificate is accurate where it matters for the distance, because leakage dominates and is tight, but its Trotter half is one to two orders of magnitude too large (Q2); and a measured nn-versus-2n2n Trotter estimate recovers nearly all of the practical cost without the ground state, at the price of being empirical (Q3). The recommended practice is therefore: certify γ\gamma, Δ\Delta and the leakage rigorously, choose nn by the hybrid rule, and report the certified count as the conservative fallback.

What it costs. At ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2} the hybrid circuit costs 2×1032\times10^{3}–4×1044\times10^{4} CX at N=6N=6–1616 against 3030–1,1001{,}100 for direct MPS preparation, and the certified circuit for N=16N=16–3232 costs 2.8×1062.8\times10^{6}–9.6×1079.6\times10^{7} CX. The filter is not competitive on these chains.

When it could matter. The filter’s cost is set by the gap and the commutator constant, not by the state’s entanglement; direct preparation’s cost steps up each time the bond dimension must double. The data show the ratio narrowing at one such step (N=16N=16, J2=0J_2=0) but not crossing. A genuine crossover would need systems where the required MPS bond dimension grows quickly with size (critical systems at larger NN, or two dimensions) and where the filter can be run without paying for dense simulation. Neither is tested here.

Limitations.

  • Inputs. The certificate needs γ\gamma and Δ\Delta, which come from DMRG. They are validated against exact diagonalisation to N=20N=20 (errors ≲4×10−13\lesssim4\times10^{-13}) and by run-to-run agreement to N=32N=32, but nothing certifies them: a rigorous lower bound on the gap is not available from DMRG (a variational run cannot bound E1E_1 from below; interlacing bounds the direction of the error, not its size), and Section 4.3 shows the certificate breaks if the gap is optimistic. The results are therefore conditional certificates (Section 2). Obtaining a lower bound on μ0\mu_0 or on the gap independently is the main open point.
  • Models. Three couplings of one chain (J2=0J_2=0, 0.24110.2411, 0.40.4) at larger NN, two at N≤16N\le16. The ground state is nondegenerate in every case computed; the theorem is stated for a ground space, but a degenerate target is not tested.
  • Interval arithmetic. The bound on η\eta is computed in double precision without outward rounding; its rounding error (≲10−14\lesssim10^{-14}) is far below the pruning tolerance, but it is not a machine-checked proof.
  • Trotter order. First order only; higher-order formulas would shrink the Trotter term but need a different constant.
  • Noise. Not simulated; the triangle inequality gives an additive noise term.
  • Cost model. Gate counts of an ideal circuit: CX (all-to-all; a line costs about 22–3×3\times more) and, for the larger chains, non-Clifford RzR_z; no TT-count and no synthesis error. The expected repetition count 1/pg1/p_g assumes independent attempts. Ancilla measurement and reset, classical feed-forward for the early abort, the native implementation of controlled Pauli rotations on a real connectivity, and any error-mitigation or error-correction overhead are not included. CX counts come from a heuristic compiler and are not minimal.
  • Simulation. Dense statevectors to N=16N=16; for N=18N=18–3232 the circuits are counted, not simulated, so only the certified bound is reported there and the hybrid and measured step counts are not available.
  • Hybrid rule. An empirical rule that assumed the 1/n1/n regime and had 25 test cases.
