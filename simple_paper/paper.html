Filtering a Matrix-Product Trial State to the Ground State with the Stetcu–Baroni Projection: A Certified Workflow, Its Accuracy and Its Cost

Draft — authors to be added

7 October 2026

Abstract

DMRG gives a matrix-product trial state and the low-lying energies of a spin chain classically, but the trial state keeps some excited-state weight. The projection filter of Stetcu, Baroni and Carlson removes it on a quantum computer using one ancilla, controlled time evolution and post-selection. We describe the complete workflow, from trial state and energies to a filter and a Trotter step count, and give an a-priori guarantee on the trace distance DD between the prepared state and the exact ground state: D≤ℓ‾+ϵT/pgD\le\sqrt{\bar\ell}+\epsilon_T/\sqrt{p_g}, a leakage term plus a Trotter term joined by the triangle inequality. We evaluate it on the open J1J_1–J2J_2 spin-½ chain (N=4N=4–1212, bond-dimension-2 trial states, exact simulation, 88 simulated points). The guarantee is never violated and lands within 2.72.7–4.9×4.9\times of the measured distance, because the leakage term is nearly tight (1.31.3–2.5×2.5\times). The Trotter term is conservative (1010–39×39\times), so the guaranteed step count is 1111–60×60\times the step count that is empirically sufficient. In gates, the guaranteed filter costs 2×1032\times10^3–6×1056\times10^5 CX and the empirically sufficient one 2×1022\times10^2–1.4×1041.4\times10^4, growing polynomially in NN (∼N4.6\sim N^{4.6}–N5.2N^{5.2} and ∼N2.8\sim N^{2.8}–N3.9N^{3.9}). The guaranteed filter costs more than exact generic state preparation at every size tested. The paper therefore documents a workflow that certifiably works and what it costs, not a resource advantage on these chains.

1. Introduction

A standard hybrid route to a ground state is to run DMRG, compile the resulting matrix-product state (MPS) into a shallow circuit, and then repair the circuit’s residual error with a quantum projection step. The Stetcu–Baroni–Carlson (SBC) projection [1], related to the rodeo algorithm [2], does this by multiplying the trial state’s energy components by a filter F(E)=∏icos(Eti+ϕi)F(E)=\prod_i\cos(Et_i+\phi_i), realised with pulses of controlled time evolution on one ancilla.

For someone deciding whether to use this, three questions matter. Does it work? How accurate is the guarantee, and what does it assume? What does it cost? This paper answers them for one concrete workflow:

  1. Section 2 states the workflow and the guarantee. Everything the practitioner needs is one formula and a recipe.
  2. Section 3 tests it. The guarantee is never violated; we show which part of it is tight and which is loose.
  3. Section 4 reports the cost in Trotter steps and CX gates, how it scales with NN, and how it compares with exact state preparation.
  4. Section 5 states what the workflow does not do.

Proofs are short and sit in the appendix. We do not claim a resource advantage; Section 4 shows there is none for the sizes studied.

2. The workflow and its guarantee

Inputs. A Hamiltonian written as a sum of terms H=∑gHgH=\sum_gH_g (here the bonds of the chain); the energies E0E_0, E1E_1, EtopE_{\mathrm{top}}; and a trial state |ψ⟩=∑kck|Ek⟩|\psi\rangle=\sum_kc_k|E_k\rangle. From these:

  • the scaled Hamiltonian Hs=(H−E0)/WH_s=(H-E_0)/W with W=Etop−E0W=E_{\mathrm{top}}-E_0, whose spectrum lies in {0}∪[Δ,1]\{0\}\cup[\Delta,1] with Δ=(E1−E0)/W\Delta=(E_1-E_0)/W;
  • the trial-state ground-state weight γ=|c0|2\gamma=|c_0|^2.

Filter. One pulse (t,ϕ)(t,\phi) applies 𝖧ae−itHs⊗ZaRza(2ϕ)𝖧a\mathsf H_{\mathrm{a}}\,e^{-itH_s\otimes Z_{\mathrm{a}}}\,\mathrm{Rz}_{\mathrm{a}}(2\phi)\,\mathsf H_{\mathrm{a}} to the system and an ancilla in |0⟩|0\rangle; its ancilla-|0⟩|0\rangle block is cos(Hst+ϕ)\cos(H_st+\phi). Keeping outcome 00 after mm pulses applies, up to normalisation, F(E)=∏i=1mcos(Eti+ϕi)F(E)=\prod_{i=1}^m\cos(Et_i+\phi_i) with total time T=∑itiT=\sum_it_i and F(0)=∏icosϕiF(0)=\prod_i\cos\phi_i. Two numbers characterise a filter: η=supE∈[Δ,1]|F(E)|/|F(0)|\eta=\sup_{E\in[\Delta,1]}|F(E)|/|F(0)| (how strongly excited states are suppressed relative to the ground state) and pg=γF(0)2p_g=\gamma F(0)^2, a lower bound on the success probability. We design (ti,ϕi)(t_i,\phi_i) with floor.py, which maximises F(0)2F(0)^2 subject to a certified upper bound on η\eta (interval bisection using |F′|≤∑|ti||F'|\le\sum|t_i|, |F″|≤(∑|ti|)2|F''|\le(\sum|t_i|)^2).

Implementation. Each controlled evolution is replaced by kik_i first-order Lie–Trotter steps over the terms HgH_g, with n=∑ikin=\sum_ik_i and ti=kidtt_i=k_i\,dt. The Trotter error is controlled by the commutator constant α=∑g∥[Hg,∑g′>gHg′]∥\alpha=\sum_g\big\|[H_g,\sum_{g'>g}H_{g'}]\big\| (the larger of the two product orders; the ancilla does not change it).

Guarantee. Let |φ̃⟩|\tilde\varphi\rangle be the implemented state, gg the ground state, and DD the trace distance. Then

D(φ̃,g)≤D‾:=ℓ‾+αT22nγF(0)2,ℓ‾=(1−γ)η2γ+(1−γ)η2\boxed{\;D(\tilde\varphi,g)\;\le\;\bar D:=\sqrt{\bar\ell}\;+\;\frac{\alpha T^2}{2n\sqrt{\gamma F(0)^2}},\qquad \bar\ell=\frac{(1-\gamma)\eta^2}{\gamma+(1-\gamma)\eta^2}\;}

The first term is leakage: what an exact filter would leave behind. It depends only on γ\gamma and η\eta. The second is the Trotter term and depends on nn. Equivalent statements: infidelity ≤D‾2\le\bar D^2 and energy error ⟨H⟩−E0≤WD‾2\langle H\rangle-E_0\le W\bar D^2. To reach a target D≤εD\le\sqrt\varepsilon with leakage budget ℓ‾≤ε/4\bar\ell\le\varepsilon/4, it suffices that n≥αT2γF(0)2ε,T=xπΔ(x=O(1) from the design),n\;\ge\;\frac{\alpha T^2}{\sqrt{\gamma F(0)^2\,\varepsilon}},\qquad T=\frac{x\pi}{\Delta}\ (x=O(1)\text{ from the design}), so the guaranteed cost scales as α/Δ2\alpha/\Delta^2. If the device is noisy and prepares a mixed state ρ̃\tilde\rho, the triangle inequality adds a noise term: D(ρ̃,g)≤D(ρ̃,φ̃)+D‾D(\tilde\rho,g)\le D(\tilde\rho,\tilde\varphi)+\bar D.

Recipe.

  1. Compute γ\gamma and Δ\Delta. If 1−γ≤ε1-\gamma\le\varepsilon the trial state already meets the target and no filter is needed.
  2. Design (ti,ϕi)(t_i,\phi_i) for a leakage budget ε/4\varepsilon/4 and certify η\eta.
  3. Compute α\alpha for the chosen term order.
  4. For each candidate nn, snap the pulses to integer step counts, re-optimise the phases, re-certify η\eta, and evaluate D‾(n)\bar D(n). Take the smallest nn with D‾(n)≤ε\bar D(n)\le\sqrt\varepsilon.
  5. Run the filter and check the observables: infidelity, energy error, and the post-selection rate.

Where the other tools in the toolbox fit. The guarantee uses the trace distance, its triangle inequality, and the fact that for pure states D=1−fidelityD=\sqrt{1-\text{fidelity}}. Pinsker’s inequality is not needed: relative entropy to a pure target is infinite, so it cannot bound DD here (Appendix B says where it does apply, and why it is weak).

3. Accuracy: what the guarantee delivers

3.1 Setup

  • System. Open spin-½ J1J_1–J2J_2 chain, J1=1J_1=1, J2∈{0,0.4}J_2\in\{0,0.4\}, N∈{4,…,12}N\in\{4,\dots,12\}. Terms are the bonds, ordered: nearest-neighbour bonds with even then odd left site, then next-nearest-neighbour bonds likewise. Exact E0,E1,EtopE_0,E_1,E_{\mathrm{top}} and the ground state come from diagonalisation.
  • Trial state. The exact ground state truncated by successive SVDs to bond dimension χ=2\chi=2, as a stand-in for a bond-dimension-2 DMRG state (γ=0.72\gamma=0.72–0.9960.996).
  • Filter. m∈{4,6}m\in\{4,6\} pulses, designed for ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2} (D≤0.1D\le0.1) with leakage budget ε/4\varepsilon/4, snapped to nn steps.
  • Measurement. Exact statevector simulation of the Trotterised filter, of the exact filter, and of the ground state. We record DD and its two components.
  • Two step counts. nboundn_{\mathrm{bound}} is the smallest nn whose guarantee is D‾≤0.1\bar D\le0.1. nmeasn_{\mathrm{meas}} is the smallest nn whose measured DD is ≤0.1\le0.1. The latter uses the exact ground state, so it is an oracle: it shows what is sufficient, not something a practitioner can know in advance.

  • Total CX at the guarantee grows as N5.2N^{5.2} (N4.6N^{4.6} at J2=0.4J_2=0.4) and at the measured step count as N3.9N^{3.9} (N2.8N^{2.8}). The guarantee costs 11–60×\times the measured count.
  • Against exact generic preparation, which grows 4×4\times per two qubits, the guaranteed filter is 131–448×\times more expensive at every size. Even the oracle measured filter is 15×15\times the generic cost at N=4N=4 and 2.2×2.2\times at N=12N=12 (J2=0J_2=0). The trend is toward a crossover just above N=12N=12 if these power laws continued and the oracle step count were available, but they are fits to five points, and exact generic preparation is a weak baseline: structured MPS preparation is cheaper still for these chains, and a companion study in this repository (evaluation/) finds no advantage for the filter at N≤12N\le12.

Summary of capability and cost. The workflow produces a filter and a step count with a proof that D≤D‾D\le\bar D, and the proof is accurate to a factor of about 3–5 in DD. What the proof costs is the Trotter term: the certified circuit is 11–60× longer than needed. The filter’s cost is set by 1/Δ21/\Delta^2 and the commutator constant, not by how entangled the state is; it can therefore only compete where state preparation itself becomes expensive, which none of these chains are.

5. Limitations

  • Inputs are assumed known. The guarantee requires γ\gamma, Δ\Delta and E0E_0. Here they come from exact diagonalisation, so everything is limited to N≤12N\le12. At larger NN DMRG supplies estimates, not bounds: the penalty-method first excitation is an upper bound on E1E_1, the wrong side for a gap lower bound, and an overlap with a DMRG state is an overlap with an approximation of |E0⟩|E_0\rangle. Certifying Δ\Delta and γ\gamma at large NN is the main open step.
  • Trial states are a stand-in. SVD truncations of the exact ground state, not compiled circuits; compilation lowers γ\gamma further. The guarantee applies unchanged given the true γ\gamma.
  • First-order Trotter only, noiseless, no gate-synthesis error.
  • Cost scope. CX only, filter only, from fits over N≤12N\le12 and two couplings.
  • Dense simulation. All checks use statevectors, so the study does not exercise DMRG’s large-NN reach.

Reproducibility: all code is in simple_paper/ (workflow.py, run_experiments.py, resources.py [needs qiskit], pinsker_check.py, make_report.py, build_paper.py); filter design is j1j2_filter/floor.py. Seeds are fixed. The N=12N=12 ground state uses a fixed Lanczos start vector because a χ=2\chi=2 cut passes through degenerate Schmidt values and γ\gamma otherwise varies in the third digit between runs. The full grid runs in a few minutes on one core.

Appendix A. Proof of the guarantee

Write D(ρ,σ)=12∥ρ−σ∥1D(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1. For pure states D=1−|⟨a|b⟩|2=sinθabD=\sqrt{1-|\langle a|b\rangle|^2}=\sin\theta_{ab}, with θab\theta_{ab} the angle between the rays. DD obeys the triangle inequality. For any state ρ\rho and pure gg, 1−⟨g|ρ|g⟩≤D(ρ,g)≤1−⟨g|ρ|g⟩1-\langle g|\rho|g\rangle\le D(\rho,g)\le\sqrt{1-\langle g|\rho|g\rangle}; for pure ρ\rho the right side is an equality, so infidelity is D2D^2. Let φ=F(Hs)ψ/∥F(Hs)ψ∥\varphi=F(H_s)\psi/\|F(H_s)\psi\| be the ideal filtered state. The theorem follows from D(φ̃,g)≤D(φ̃,φ)+D(φ,g)D(\tilde\varphi,g)\le D(\tilde\varphi,\varphi)+D(\varphi,g) and two lemmas.

Lemma 1 (leakage). D(φ,g)2=ℓ≤ℓ‾D(\varphi,g)^2=\ell\le\bar\ell. Proof. The ground state sits at E=0E=0, so |⟨g|φ⟩|2=γF(0)2/(γF(0)2+y)|\langle g|\varphi\rangle|^2=\gamma F(0)^2/(\gamma F(0)^2+y) with y=∑k≥1|ck|2F(Ek)2y=\sum_{k\ge1}|c_k|^2F(E_k)^2. All EkE_k, k≥1k\ge1, lie in [Δ,1][\Delta,1], so F(Ek)2≤η2F(0)2F(E_k)^2\le\eta^2F(0)^2 and y≤(1−γ)η2F(0)2y\le(1-\gamma)\eta^2F(0)^2. Since ℓ=y/(γF(0)2+y)\ell=y/(\gamma F(0)^2+y) is increasing in yy, the bound follows. ▫\square

Lemma 2 (Trotter). D(φ̃,φ)≤min{1,ϵT/pg}D(\tilde\varphi,\varphi)\le\min\{1,\epsilon_T/\sqrt{p_g}\} with ϵT=∑iαti2/(2ki)=αT2/(2n)\epsilon_T=\sum_i\alpha t_i^2/(2k_i)=\alpha T^2/(2n). Proof. Let Ai=⟨0a|𝒲i|0a⟩A_i=\langle0_{\mathrm{a}}|\mathcal W_i|0_{\mathrm{a}}\rangle and Ãi\tilde A_i the Trotterised block; a=Am⋯A1ψa=A_m\cdots A_1\psi, b=Ãm⋯Ã1ψb=\tilde A_m\cdots\tilde A_1\psi. The first-order product-formula bound (below) gives ∥𝒲i−𝒲̃i∥≤αti2/(2ki)\|\mathcal W_i-\widetilde{\mathcal W}_i\|\le\alpha t_i^2/(2k_i); the blocks are compressions, so ∥Ai−Ãi∥\|A_i-\tilde A_i\| is no larger, and all blocks have norm ≤1\le1. Telescoping, a−b=∑i(Am⋯Ai+1)(Ai−Ãi)(Ãi−1⋯Ã1ψ)a-b=\sum_i(A_m\cdots A_{i+1})(A_i-\tilde A_i)(\tilde A_{i-1}\cdots\tilde A_1\psi), so ∥a−b∥≤ϵT\|a-b\|\le\epsilon_T. Corollaries. Infidelity =D2≤D‾2=D^2\le\bar D^2. Energy: with c̃k\tilde c_k the components of φ̃\tilde\varphi, ⟨H⟩−E0=∑k≥1|c̃k|2(Ek−E0)≤W(1−|c̃0|2)=WD2\langle H\rangle-E_0=\sum_{k\ge1}|\tilde c_k|^2(E_k-E_0)\le W(1-|\tilde c_0|^2)=WD^2.

First-order Trotter bound. Appendix B. Where Pinsker’s inequality applies

For the target |g⟩⟨g||g\rangle\langle g| the relative entropy S(ρ∥|g⟩⟨g|)S(\rho\|\,|g\rangle\langle g|) is infinite unless ρ=|g⟩⟨g|\rho=|g\rangle\langle g|, so D≤S/2D\le\sqrt{S/2} gives nothing here. It does apply to the energy-resolved distributions pk=|ck|2p_k=|c_k|^2 (trial) and qk=pkF(Ek)2/Psuccq_k=p_kF(E_k)^2/P_{\mathrm{succ}} (filtered): KL(q∥p)=∑kqkln(F(Ek)2/Psucc)≤ln(1/Psucc)\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)=\sum_kq_k\ln(F(E_k)^2/P_{\mathrm{succ}})\le\ln(1/P_{\mathrm{succ}}), and TV(p,q)≥q0−p0=1−ℓ−γ\mathrm{TV}(p,q)\ge q_0-p_0=1-\ell-\gamma, so with TV≤KL/2\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}/2}, Psucc≤exp(−2(1−ℓ−γ)2).P_{\mathrm{succ}}\le\exp\!\big(-2(1-\ell-\gamma)^2\big). This is a necessary condition on the post-selection cost, not an error bound. We checked it on the five ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2} cases with N≤8N\le8: all inequalities held, but the cap (0.950.95–1.001.00) is far above the actual PsuccP_{\mathrm{succ}} (0.360.36–0.940.94), so it is uninformative for these trial states.
