A Trace-Distance Error Bound for Stetcu–Baroni Filtering of a Matrix-Product Trial State

Draft — authors to be added

7 October 2026

Abstract

A common hybrid recipe for ground-state preparation is to obtain a matrix-product trial state and the low-lying energies classically (DMRG), then repair the trial state’s residual excited-state weight on a quantum computer with the ancilla-based projection filter of Stetcu, Baroni and Carlson. We give a short, fully explicit a-priori error bound for this recipe. Writing DD for the trace distance between the prepared state and the exact ground state |E0⟩|E_0\rangle, the bound is a sum of two terms obtained from the triangle inequality: a leakage term, set by the trial-state ground-state weight γ\gamma and a certified suppression factor η\eta of the filter on the excited spectrum, and a Trotter term, set by a first-order commutator constant α\alpha, the total evolution time TT and the number of Trotter steps nn. The bound needs only the trial state’s overlap, the spectral gap, the filter and the Hamiltonian’s term structure, and it converts to bounds on infidelity (D2D^2) and on the energy error (WD2W D^2). We test it on the open spin-½ J1J_1–J2J_2 chain for N=4N=4–1212 with bond-dimension-2 trial states, by exact simulation of the Trotterised filter. All 88 simulated points satisfy every inequality. At the step count the bound prescribes for a target D≤0.1D\le 0.1, it overestimates the measured DD by 2.72.7–4.9×4.9\times: the leakage term is nearly tight (1.31.3–2.5×2.5\times) while the Trotter term is conservative (1010–39×39\times), so the bound asks for 1111–60×60\times more Trotter steps than are empirically needed. Translated to two-qubit gates, the filter costs 2×1032\times10^3–6×1056\times10^5 CX at the bound’s step count (2×1022\times10^2–1.4×1041.4\times10^4 at the measured step count), growing roughly as N4.6N^{4.6}–N5.2N^{5.2} (bound) and N2.8N^{2.8}–N3.9N^{3.9} (measured) over N=6N=6–1212, against a 4×4\times increase per two qubits for exact generic state preparation. We also show where Pinsker’s inequality does and does not enter.

1. Introduction

DMRG gives, classically and cheaply, an accurate matrix-product state (MPS) and estimates of the low-lying energies of a one-dimensional spin chain. An MPS of modest bond dimension can be compiled into a shallow circuit, but the resulting trial state |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle has ground-state weight γ=|⟨E0|ψ⟩|2<1\gamma=|\langle E_0|\psi\rangle|^2<1. The projection algorithm of Stetcu, Baroni and Carlson [1] (related to the rodeo algorithm [2]) repairs this with one ancilla, controlled time evolution under the Hamiltonian, and post-selection; the effect is to multiply the trial state’s energy components by a filter F(E)=∏icos(Eti+ϕi)F(E)=\prod_i\cos(Et_i+\phi_i).

The question this note answers is the practical one: given a trial state, the energies and a filter, how close is the prepared state to |E0⟩|E_0\rangle, and how many Trotter steps are enough? We want an answer that (i) is a theorem, (ii) uses only quantities a practitioner has, (iii) fits in a few lines, and (iv) comes with empirical data showing how conservative it is.

Contributions.

  1. A two-term bound D≤ℓ‾+ϵT/pgD\le\sqrt{\bar\ell}+\epsilon_T/\sqrt{p_g} on the trace distance to the ground state (Theorem 1), proved from three elementary facts: trace distance of pure states is 1−|⟨⋅|⋅⟩|2\sqrt{1-|\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle|^2}; it obeys the triangle inequality; and the leakage and Trotter errors can each be bounded separately. Corollaries give infidelity, energy error, and the number of Trotter steps.
  2. An end-to-end workflow (Section 4) from trial state and energies to a certified step count.
  3. An empirical study (Section 5): the bound is never violated, and we quantify which of its two terms is loose.
  4. CX-count resource scaling of the certified and the measured step counts, on all-to-all and line connectivity (Section 5.8).
  5. A short account of where Pinsker’s inequality fits (Section 3.5), including why it cannot replace the triangle-inequality argument for a pure target.

What is not claimed. The bound is first-order-Trotter only and assumes the gap, γ\gamma and the ground energy are known (here: from exact diagonalisation, N≤12N\le12). Hardware noise and gate synthesis are not modelled. Section 6 lists the consequences. Resource counts are CX only (no T counts or synthesis error), noiseless, for the filter alone (the trial-state circuit is not included). A companion cost study in this repository (evaluation/) compares the filter with direct MPS-circuit preparation and finds no resource advantage for 1D chains with N≤12N\le12; Section 5.8 uses a weaker baseline (exact generic state preparation) and does not revisit that conclusion.

3. The bound

3.1 Trace distance, the triangle inequality, and fidelity

For states ρ,σ\rho,\sigma let D(ρ,σ)=12∥ρ−σ∥1=12Tr|ρ−σ|D(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}|\rho-\sigma|. Three facts are used.

  • (D1) Pure states. D(|a⟩⟨a|,|b⟩⟨b|)=1−|⟨a|b⟩|2=sinθabD(|a\rangle\langle a|,|b\rangle\langle b|)=\sqrt{1-|\langle a|b\rangle|^2}=\sin\theta_{ab}, where θab\theta_{ab} is the angle between the rays.
  • (D2) Triangle inequality. D(ρ,τ)≤D(ρ,σ)+D(σ,τ)D(\rho,\tau)\le D(\rho,\sigma)+D(\sigma,\tau), since the trace norm is a norm.
  • (D3) Fidelity (Fuchs–van de Graaf type). For any state ρ\rho and pure |g⟩|g\rangle, 1−⟨g|ρ|g⟩≤D(ρ,|g⟩⟨g|)≤1−⟨g|ρ|g⟩\;1-\langle g|\rho|g\rangle\le D(\rho,|g\rangle\langle g|)\le\sqrt{1-\langle g|\rho|g\rangle}. For pure ρ\rho the right-hand side is an equality, so infidelity =D2=D^2.

The lower bound in (D3) holds because D≥Tr[|g⟩⟨g|(σ−ρ)]D\ge\operatorname{Tr}[|g\rangle\langle g|(\sigma-\rho)] with σ=|g⟩⟨g|\sigma=|g\rangle\langle g|.

3.2 Leakage: how well would an exact filter do?

Lemma 1 (leakage). If F(0)≠0F(0)\ne0, the ideal filtered state satisfies ℓ:=1−|⟨g|φ⟩|2≤ℓ‾:=(1−γ)η2γ+(1−γ)η2,soD(φ,g)=ℓ≤ℓ‾.\ell:=1-|\langle g|\varphi\rangle|^2\;\le\;\bar\ell:=\frac{(1-\gamma)\,\eta^2}{\gamma+(1-\gamma)\,\eta^2},\qquad\text{so}\qquad D(\varphi,g)=\sqrt{\ell}\le\sqrt{\bar\ell}.

Proof. The ground state sits at E=0E=0 in the scaled spectrum, so |⟨g|φ⟩|2=γF(0)2/(γF(0)2+y)|\langle g|\varphi\rangle|^2=\gamma F(0)^2/(\gamma F(0)^2+y) with y=∑k≥1|ck|2F(Ek)2y=\sum_{k\ge1}|c_k|^2F(E_k)^2. All EkE_k, k≥1k\ge1, lie in [Δ,1][\Delta,1], so F(Ek)2≤η2F(0)2F(E_k)^2\le\eta^2F(0)^2 and y≤(1−γ)η2F(0)2y\le(1-\gamma)\eta^2F(0)^2. Since ℓ=y/(γF(0)2+y)\ell=y/(\gamma F(0)^2+y) is increasing in yy, substituting the bound for yy gives the claim. The equality D=ℓD=\sqrt\ell is (D1). ▫\square

η\eta is certified rather than sampled: |F′|≤L:=∑i|ti||F'|\le L:=\sum_i|t_i| and |F″|≤L2|F''|\le L^2, so on an interval of half-width dd about EcE_c, |F(E)|≤|F(Ec)|+|F′(Ec)|d+12L2d2|F(E)|\le|F(E_c)|+|F'(E_c)|d+\tfrac12L^2d^2; interval bisection with this envelope gives a rigorous upper bound on sup[Δ,1]|F|\sup_{[\Delta,1]}|F| (floor.certify).

3.3 Trotter error of the post-selected state

Lemma 2 (Trotter). Let Ai=⟨0a|𝒲(ti,ϕi)|0a⟩A_i=\langle0_{\mathrm{a}}|\mathcal W(t_i,\phi_i)|0_{\mathrm{a}}\rangle and Ãi\tilde A_i the same block with kik_i Trotter steps, and set a=Am⋯A1ψa=A_m\cdots A_1\psi, b=Ãm⋯Ã1ψb=\tilde A_m\cdots\tilde A_1\psi (un-normalised). Proof. (i) The first-order product-formula bound (Appendix A, following Childs et al. [3]) gives ∥𝒲i−𝒲̃i∥≤αti2/(2ki)\|\mathcal W_i-\widetilde{\mathcal W}_i\|\le\alpha t_i^2/(2k_i). (ii) Ai,ÃiA_i,\tilde A_i are compressions of 𝒲i,𝒲̃i\mathcal W_i,\widetilde{\mathcal W}_i to the ancilla-|0⟩|0\rangle block, so ∥Ai−Ãi∥≤∥𝒲i−𝒲̃i∥\|A_i-\tilde A_i\|\le\|\mathcal W_i-\widetilde{\mathcal W}_i\|, and ∥Ai∥,∥Ãi∥≤1\|A_i\|,\|\tilde A_i\|\le1. (iii) Telescoping, a−b=∑i(Am⋯Ai+1)(Ai−Ãi)(Ãi−1⋯Ã1ψ)a-b=\sum_i(A_m\cdots A_{i+1})(A_i-\tilde A_i)(\tilde A_{i-1}\cdots\tilde A_1\psi), so ∥a−b∥≤∑iϵi\|a-b\|\le\sum_i\epsilon_i. (iv) The rays of aa and bb are φ\varphi and φ̃\tilde\varphi. By (D1), D=sin∠(a,b)=dist(a,spanb)/∥a∥≤∥a−b∥/∥a∥D=\sin\angle(a,b)=\operatorname{dist}(a,\operatorname{span}b)/\|a\|\le\|a-b\|/\|a\|, and ∥a∥2=Psucc≥pg\|a\|^2=P_{\mathrm{succ}}\ge p_g. ▫\square

3.4 The bound

Theorem 1. Under the assumptions of Section 2, D(φ̃,g)≤D‾:=min{1,ℓ‾+αT22nγF(0)2}\boxed{\;D(\tilde\varphi,g)\;\le\;\bar D:=\min\Big\{1,\;\sqrt{\bar\ell}+\frac{\alpha T^2}{2n\sqrt{\gamma F(0)^2}}\Big\}\;}

Proof. Triangle inequality (D2): D(φ̃,g)≤D(φ̃,φ)+D(φ,g)D(\tilde\varphi,g)\le D(\tilde\varphi,\varphi)+D(\varphi,g). Bound the first term by Lemma 2 and the second by Lemma 1. ▫\square

Corollary 1 (infidelity). 1−|⟨g|φ̃⟩|2=D2≤D‾21-|\langle g|\tilde\varphi\rangle|^2=D^2\le\bar D^2.

Corollary 2 (energy). ⟨φ̃|H|φ̃⟩−E0≤WD2≤WD‾2\langle\tilde\varphi|H|\tilde\varphi\rangle-E_0\le W\,D^2\le W\bar D^2. Proof. With c̃k\tilde c_k the components of φ̃\tilde\varphi, ∑k≥1|c̃k|2(Ek−E0)≤W∑k≥1|c̃k|2=W(1−|c̃0|2)=WD2\sum_{k\ge1}|\tilde c_k|^2(E_k-E_0)\le W\sum_{k\ge1}|\tilde c_k|^2=W(1-|\tilde c_0|^2)=WD^2. ▫\square

Corollary 3 (step count). For a target D≤εD\le\sqrt\varepsilon and a certified ℓ‾<ε\bar\ell<\varepsilon, it suffices that n≥αT22γF(0)2(ε−ℓ‾),e.g. n≥αT2γF(0)2ε if ℓ‾≤ε/4.n\;\ge\;\frac{\alpha T^2}{2\sqrt{\gamma F(0)^2}\,\big(\sqrt\varepsilon-\sqrt{\bar\ell}\big)},\qquad\text{e.g. }\ n\ge\frac{\alpha T^2}{\sqrt{\gamma F(0)^2\,\varepsilon}}\ \text{ if }\bar\ell\le\varepsilon/4 . The design problem is then: choose (ti,ϕi)(t_i,\phi_i) to maximise F(0)2F(0)^2 (hence pgp_g and PsuccP_{\mathrm{succ}}) subject to certified η≤η*\eta\le\eta_*, where η*2=γεℓ/((1−εℓ)(1−γ))\eta_*^2=\gamma\varepsilon_\ell/((1-\varepsilon_\ell)(1-\gamma)) makes ℓ‾=εℓ\bar\ell=\varepsilon_\ell. 3.5 Remarks: noise, tightness, and Pinsker

Mixed states and noise. If the device prepares a mixed state ρ̃\tilde\rho (e.g. with noise), (D2) still applies: D(ρ̃,g)≤D(ρ̃,φ̃)+D‾D(\tilde\rho,g)\le D(\tilde\rho,\tilde\varphi)+\bar D. The infidelity bound then degrades from D2D^2 to DD via (D3).

Sharpening. With angles instead of sines, D≤sin(arcsinℓ‾+arcsin(ϵT/pg))D\le\sin\!\big(\arcsin\sqrt{\bar\ell}+\arcsin(\epsilon_T/\sqrt{p_g})\big) whenever the sum of angles is at most π/2\pi/2. It is slightly tighter and no harder to compute; we keep the additive form because it is the one that reads off as “leakage plus Trotter”.

Pinsker. For the target |g⟩⟨g||g\rangle\langle g|, Pinsker’s inequality D≤S(ρ∥σ)/2D\le\sqrt{S(\rho\|\sigma)/2} is vacuous in the useful direction: S(ρ∥|g⟩⟨g|)=+∞S(\rho\|\,|g\rangle\langle g|)=+\infty unless ρ=|g⟩⟨g|\rho=|g\rangle\langle g|, because ρ\rho has support outside a pure state. This is why the argument above goes through fidelity and the triangle inequality rather than relative entropy. Pinsker does enter if one looks at the energy-resolved distributions pk=|ck|2p_k=|c_k|^2 (trial) and qk=pkF(Ek)2/Psuccq_k=p_kF(E_k)^2/P_{\mathrm{succ}} (ideally filtered), for which

KL(q∥p)=∑kqklnF(Ek)2Psucc≤ln1Psucc(|F|≤1),TV(p,q)≤12KL(q∥p),\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)=\sum_kq_k\ln\frac{F(E_k)^2}{P_{\mathrm{succ}}}\le\ln\frac1{P_{\mathrm{succ}}}\quad(|F|\le1),\qquad \mathrm{TV}(p,q)\le\sqrt{\tfrac12\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)},

while TV(p,q)≥q0−p0=1−ℓ−γ\mathrm{TV}(p,q)\ge q_0-p_0=1-\ell-\gamma. Together, Psucc≤exp(−2(1−ℓ−γ)2):P_{\mathrm{succ}}\;\le\;\exp\!\big(-2(1-\ell-\gamma)^2\big): post-selection cannot be free if the filter is to move weight from γ\gamma to 1−ℓ1-\ell. This is a necessary condition on the cost, not an error bound, and (Section 5.8) it is weak for the cases studied. We include it to mark where relative-entropy tools apply and to be explicit that they do not drive the main result.

4. The workflow

  1. Inputs. The Hamiltonian grouped as ∑gHg\sum_gH_g; E0,E1,EtopE_0,E_1,E_{\mathrm{top}}; the trial state |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle (statevector or circuit).
  2. Scale. W=Etop−E0W=E_{\mathrm{top}}-E_0 (or any certified upper bound), HsH_s, Δ\Delta.
  3. Overlap. γ=|⟨E0|ψ⟩|2\gamma=|\langle E_0|\psi\rangle|^2. If 1−γ≤ε1-\gamma\le\varepsilon, the trial state already meets the target (D=1−γ≤εD=\sqrt{1-\gamma}\le\sqrt\varepsilon) and no filter is needed.
  4. Filter. Choose a leakage budget εℓ\varepsilon_\ell (we use ε/4\varepsilon/4); solve for (ti,ϕi)(t_i,\phi_i) maximising F(0)2F(0)^2 with certified η≤η*\eta\le\eta_*; obtain TT.
  5. Commutator constant. Compute α\alpha for the chosen term order (both orders, take the max).
  6. Step count. For each candidate nn: snap the pulses to integer step counts kik_i with ti=kidtt_i=k_i\,dt, re-optimise the phases and re-certify η\eta, evaluate D‾(n)\bar D(n) of Theorem 1; take the smallest nn with D‾(n)≤ε\bar D(n)\le\sqrt\varepsilon.
  7. Run and check. Simulate or run the Trotterised filter; compare the observables the bound speaks about: infidelity, ⟨H⟩−E0\langle H\rangle-E_0, and the post-selection rate Psucc≥γF(0)2P_{\mathrm{succ}}\ge\gamma F(0)^2.

5. Empirical study

5.1 Set-up

  • System. Open spin-½ J1J_1–J2J_2 chain, J1=1J_1=1, J2∈{0,0.4}J_2\in\{0,0.4\}, N∈{4,6,8,10,12}N\in\{4,6,8,10,12\}. Terms HgH_g are the bonds, in the order: nearest-neighbour bonds with even left site, with odd left site, then next-nearest-neighbour bonds likewise.   • Trial state. The exact ground state truncated by successive SVDs to MPS bond dimension χ=2\chi=2 (a stand-in for a bond-dimension-2 DMRG state; γ=0.72\gamma=0.72–0.9960.996). Section 5.6 varies χ\chi at N=8N=8.
  • Filter. floor.py designs: m∈{4,6}m\in\{4,6\} pulses, x=TΔ/π∈{0.6,1,1.5,2}x=T\Delta/\pi\in\{0.6,1,1.5,2\}, leakage budget εℓ=ε/4\varepsilon_\ell=\varepsilon/4, η\eta certified by interval bisection, then snapped to nn steps with phases re-optimised and η\eta re-certified.
  • Targets. ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2} (D≤0.1D\le0.1) and ε=10−1\varepsilon=10^{-1} (D≤0.316D\le0.316).
  • Measurement. Exact statevector simulation of the Trotterised filter (ancilla branches ±\pm simulated separately, the constant shift included in the phases), and of the exact filter via sparse matrix exponentials. We record D(φ̃,g)D(\tilde\varphi,g), D(φ,g)D(\varphi,g) and D(φ̃,φ)D(\tilde\varphi,\varphi), the energy error, and PsuccP_{\mathrm{succ}}.
  • Two step counts. nboundn_{\mathrm{bound}} is the smallest nn with D‾(n)≤ε\bar D(n)\le\sqrt\varepsilon (Section 4, step 6). nempn_{\mathrm{emp}} is the smallest nn on a geometric grid with measured D≤εD\le\sqrt\varepsilon. It uses the exact ground state, so it is an oracle, not something a practitioner has.

5.5 Dependence on nn

Figure 3 shows the sweep. 5.8 Pinsker numerics

For χ=2\chi=2, ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2} and (N,J2)∈{(4,0),(6,0),(8,0),(6,0.4),(8,0.4)}(N,J_2)\in\{(4,0),(6,0),(8,0),(6,0.4),(8,0.4)\}, the energy-resolved quantities of Section 3.5 (computed for the continuous filter designs and the exact filter) satisfy KL(q∥p)≤ln(1/Psucc)\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)\le\ln(1/P_{\mathrm{succ}}) and TV(p,q)≤KL/2\mathrm{TV}(p,q)\le\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}/2} in every case, as they must. The implied cap on the success probability, exp(−2(1−ℓ−γ)2)\exp(-2(1-\ell-\gamma)^2), is 0.950.95–1.001.00, against actual PsuccP_{\mathrm{succ}} between 0.360.36 and 0.940.94. It is true but too weak to be informative for these trial states, which already have large γ\gamma. We therefore treat Pinsker as an explanatory remark, not as part of the guarantee.

6. Discussion and limitations

  • Inputs. The bound is conditional on exact γ\gamma and Δ\Delta (and E0E_0 for the shift). Here they come from exact diagonalisation, so everything is restricted to N≤12N\le12. At larger NN the energies come from DMRG, which gives estimates, not bounds: the penalty-method first-excitation energy is an upper bound on E1E_1, the wrong side for a gap lower bound, and an overlap with a DMRG state is an overlap with an approximation of |E0⟩|E_0\rangle. Certifying Δ\Delta and γ\gamma at large NN is the main open point for turning this into a large-NN workflow.
  • Trial state. The trial states are SVD truncations of the exact ground state standing in for DMRG/MPS-circuit states. Circuit compilation would reduce γ\gamma further; the bound applies unchanged given the true γ\gamma.
  • First order only. Lemma 2 is a first-order product-formula statement.
  • Conservatism. The Trotter term is the loose one (10–39×). Using the Richardson estimate in its place gives a practical, non-rigorous complement.
  • Noise. Not modelled; (D2) adds a noise term as in Section 3.5.
  • Cost. CX counts are for the filter alone, noiseless, without T-count or synthesis error, and from short power-law fits (N≤12N\le12). We make no claim of advantage over direct MPS-circuit preparation (see the companion study).
  • Scale. N≤12N\le12, two couplings, dense simulation.

7. Reproducibility

Everything is in simple_paper/: workflow.py (Hamiltonian, trial state, pulses, bound), run_experiments.py (cases, writes results/case_*.json), pinsker_check.py, resources.py (CX counts; the only script that needs qiskit), make_report.py (tables, figures), build_paper.py (this document). The filter design and certification are j1j2_filter/floor.py. Requirements: numpy, scipy, matplotlib; resources.py additionally needs qiskit. No quimb or mps-to-circuit. The full grid takes a few minutes on one CPU core (the N=12N=12 cases dominate). Seeds are fixed (the N=12N=12 ground state uses a fixed Lanczos start vector, since χ=2\chi=2 truncation cuts through degenerate Schmidt values and γ\gamma otherwise varies in the third digit between runs).

Appendix A. First-order Trotter bound

Two terms. For Hermitian A,BA,B, U(s)=e−is(A+B)U(s)=e^{-is(A+B)} and V(s)=e−isAe−isBV(s)=e^{-isA}e^{-isB}: dds[U(δ−s)V(s)]=iU(δ−s)(B−e−isABeisA)V(s)\frac{d}{ds}[U(\delta-s)V(s)]=iU(\delta-s)\big(B-e^{-isA}Be^{isA}\big)V(s), so integrating from 00 to δ\delta, U(δ)−V(δ)=−i∫0δU(δ−s)(B−e−isABeisA)V(s)ds.U(\delta)-V(\delta)=-i\int_0^\delta U(\delta-s)\big(B-e^{-isA}Be^{isA}\big)V(s)\,ds . Since ddse−isABeisA=−ie−isA[A,B]eisA\frac{d}{ds}e^{-isA}Be^{isA}=-ie^{-isA}[A,B]e^{isA}, we have ∥B−e−isABeisA∥≤s∥[A,B]∥\|B-e^{-isA}Be^{isA}\|\le s\|[A,B]\|, and U,VU,V are unitary, so ∥U(δ)−V(δ)∥≤δ22∥[A,B]∥\|U(\delta)-V(\delta)\|\le\tfrac{\delta^2}{2}\|[A,B]\|.

Many terms. For H=H1+…+HΓH=H_1+\dots+H_\Gamma and the product S=e−iδH1⋯e−iδHΓS=e^{-i\delta H_1}\cdots e^{-i\delta H_\Gamma}, apply the two-term bound with A=H1A=H_1, B=R1:=∑g>1HgB=R_1:=\sum_{g>1}H_g and recurse on e−iδR1e^{-i\delta R_1} versus the product of the rest (unitary invariance of the norm absorbs the leading factor): ∥e−iδH−S∥≤δ22∑g∥[Hg,∑g′>gHg′]∥=δ22α.\big\|e^{-i\delta H}-S\big\|\le\frac{\delta^2}{2}\sum_{g}\Big\|\Big[H_g,\sum_{g'>g}H_{g'}\Big]\Big\|=\frac{\delta^2}{2}\alpha . With δ=t/k\delta=t/k and kk steps, ∥Xk−Yk∥≤k∥X−Y∥\|X^k-Y^k\|\le k\|X-Y\| gives αt2/(2k)\alpha t^2/(2k). The opposite product order gives the reversed sum; the code takes the larger of the two. This is the first-order case of Childs et al. [3].

Ancilla. For Hs⊗ZH_s\otimes Z the terms are HgZH_gZ, and [HgZ,Hg′Z]=[Hg,Hg′]⊗Z2=[Hg,Hg′]⊗𝟙[H_gZ,H_{g'}Z]=[H_g,H_{g'}]\otimes Z^2=[H_g,H_{g'}]\otimes\mathbb 1, so α\alpha is unchanged. The constant shift −E0/W-E_0/W commutes with everything and is applied exactly.
